# Before and After Examples

Worked examples of AGSM violations and corrections. Each example shows the original text, the corrected version, and the rule that applies.

Rule IDs correspond to entries in `schema/ruleset.json` and `rules/`.

---

## Grammar and Punctuation

### Greengrocer's apostrophe (apostrophe-no-plural-nouns)

❌ **Before:** The agency processed 1,200 FOI's in the 2022–23 financial year.
✅ **After:** The agency processed 1,200 FOIs in the 2022–23 financial year.

*Apostrophes never form the plural of nouns or acronyms. `FOIs` — no apostrophe.*

---

### it's vs its (apostrophe-its-its-confusion)

❌ **Before:** The committee released it's final report on Monday.
✅ **After:** The committee released its final report on Monday.

*`it's` = it is / it has. `its` = possessive. No apostrophe for possession.*

---

### Comma splice (comma-no-splice)

❌ **Before:** The consultation period closes on 30 June, submissions received after that date will not be considered.
✅ **After:** The consultation period closes on 30 June. Submissions received after that date will not be considered.

*Two independent sentences cannot be joined by a comma alone. Use a full stop, or add a conjunction.*

---

### Em dash instead of spaced en dash (dash-spaced-en-not-em)

❌ **Before:** The review—which took eight months—found no evidence of wrongdoing.
✅ **After:** The review – which took eight months – found no evidence of wrongdoing.

*Australian government style uses a spaced en dash (–) for parenthetical asides, not an unspaced em dash (—).*

---

### Coordinate nouns: hyphen instead of en dash (dash-coordinate-nouns-en-not-hyphen)

❌ **Before:** The Murray-Darling Basin Authority released its water outlook.
✅ **After:** The Murray–Darling Basin Authority released its water outlook.

*Coordinate nouns (both retain their full meaning) use an en dash, not a hyphen.*

---

### Acronym used without definition (acronym-define-first-use)

❌ **Before:** ATSIC was abolished in 2005.
✅ **After:** The Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Commission (ATSIC) was abolished in 2005.

*Define every acronym on first use. Write the full term first, then the short form in parentheses.*

---

### Full stops inside acronym (acronym-no-full-stop)

❌ **Before:** The A.T.O. processes millions of tax returns each year.
✅ **After:** The ATO processes millions of tax returns each year.

*No full stops within or after acronyms and initialisms.*

---

### Comma after Latin abbreviation (comma-no-latin-abbreviation)

❌ **Before:** Suitable items (e.g., a passport, a birth certificate) must be provided.
✅ **After:** Suitable items (e.g. a passport, a birth certificate) must be provided.

*No comma after Latin shortened forms inside parentheses.*

---

### Semicolons at end of list items (semicolon-no-list-items)

❌ **Before:**
- submit your application form;
- attach supporting documents;
- pay the application fee.

✅ **After:**
- submit your application form
- attach supporting documents
- pay the application fee.

*List items must not end with semicolons. The full stop appears only on the final item (if it's a fragment list).*

---

## Numbers and Dates

### US date format (date-format-day-month-year)

❌ **Before:** The consultation period ends December 31, 2026.
✅ **After:** The consultation period ends 31 December 2026.

*Australian date format: day month year, no commas, month spelled in full.*

---

### Ordinal suffix in a full date (date-no-ordinal-suffix-in-full-dates)

❌ **Before:** Submissions are due by 1st March 2026.
✅ **After:** Submissions are due by 1 March 2026.

*When a month is present, write the day as a plain numeral — no ordinal suffix.*

---

### 'percent' instead of 'per cent' (percentage-per-cent-two-words)

❌ **Before:** The unemployment rate fell by 0.3 percent last quarter.
✅ **After:** The unemployment rate fell by 0.3 per cent last quarter.

*Australian English: `per cent` — two words.*

---

### Space before % sign (percentage-numeral-and-symbol-no-space)

❌ **Before:** The approval rate was 94 % for the reporting period.
✅ **After:** The approval rate was 94% for the reporting period.

*No space between the numeral and the % symbol.*

---

### Missing leading zero on decimal (decimal-leading-zero)

❌ **Before:** The margin of error was ±.05 percentage points.
✅ **After:** The margin of error was ±0.05 percentage points.

*Always include a leading zero before the decimal point for values less than 1.*

---

### Time: full stop instead of colon (time-colon-separator)

❌ **Before:** The hearing commences at 10.30 am.
✅ **After:** The hearing commences at 10:30 am.

*Use a colon, not a full stop, to separate hours and minutes.*

---

### 'AM' instead of 'am' (time-am-pm-lowercase)

❌ **Before:** Registration opens at 8:30 AM.
✅ **After:** Registration opens at 8:30 am.

*`am` and `pm` are always lowercase.*

---

### Ambiguous 12 pm (time-noon-midday-midnight)

❌ **Before:** The closing time for submissions is 12 pm.
✅ **After:** The closing time for submissions is noon.

*`12 am` and `12 pm` are ambiguous. Use `noon`, `midday`, or `midnight`.*

---

### Financial year: hyphen instead of en dash (financial-year-en-dash)

❌ **Before:** The department's 2023-24 budget increased by 12%.
✅ **After:** The department's 2023–24 budget increased by 12%.

*Financial years use an en dash (–), not a hyphen (-).*

---

### Space in currency (currency-symbol-no-space-before-numeral)

❌ **Before:** The grant amount is $ 50,000 per project.
✅ **After:** The grant amount is $50,000 per project.

*No space between the currency symbol and the numeral.*

---

### Imperial units (measurement-avoid-imperial)

❌ **Before:** The trail is approximately 3 miles long.
✅ **After:** The trail is approximately 5 km long.

*Australia uses metric (SI) units. No imperial units in current Australian government content.*

---

### Plural on unit symbol (measurement-no-plural-s-on-symbols)

❌ **Before:** The package weighs 12 kgs and must be transported within 48 hrs.
✅ **After:** The package weighs 12 kg and must be transported within 48 hours.

*Unit symbols serve as both singular and plural — no added 's'.*

---

## Capitalisation

### Title case in a heading (headings-sentence-case)

❌ **Before:** ## How To Apply For A Community Grant
✅ **After:** ## How to apply for a community grant

*Sentence case for all headings — only the first word, proper nouns, and acronyms are capitalised.*

---

### 'government' incorrectly capitalised (government-word-in-names)

❌ **Before:** The Australian Government announced the initiative. The Government will review it annually.
✅ **After:** The Australian Government announced the initiative. The government will review it annually.

*`Australian Government` (formal name) takes both capitals. Subsequent generic references use lowercase `government`.*

---

### 'Commonwealth government' (commonwealth-capitalisation)

❌ **Before:** Commonwealth government policy requires all agencies to report annually.
✅ **After:** Australian Government policy requires all agencies to report annually.

*`Commonwealth government` is incorrect. Use `Australian Government`.*

---

### Generic 'the Minister' capitalised (minister-title-caps)

❌ **Before:** The Minister for Education announced the initiative. Education groups will meet with the Minister.
✅ **After:** The Minister for Education announced the initiative. Education groups will meet with the minister.

*Complete official titles before a name: capitalised. Generic subsequent references: lowercase.*

---

### 'Act' lowercase (legislation-act-capitalisation)

❌ **Before:** The act separates the two entities.
✅ **After:** The Act separates the two entities.

*`Act` is always capitalised when referring to legislation.*

---

## Plain English

### Nominalisation (avoid-nominalisation)

❌ **Before:** Please make an application before the closing date in order to receive consideration of your request.
✅ **After:** Please apply before the closing date so we can consider your request.

*Convert verb-derived nouns back to verbs: apply (not *make an application*), consider (not *give consideration to*).*

---

### Formal vocabulary (everyday-words-preferred)

❌ **Before:** Applicants should endeavour to ascertain their eligibility prior to commencing the application process.
✅ **After:** Check your eligibility before you start the application.

*Plain words: check (not ascertain), before (not prior to), start (not commence).*

---

### Passive voice (active-voice-preferred)

❌ **Before:** Applications will be assessed by the department within 30 days of receipt. Successful applicants will be notified.
✅ **After:** The department will assess applications within 30 days of receipt. We will notify successful applicants.

*Active voice: the subject performs the action. Who does what is immediately clear.*

---

### Third-person reference to reader (second-person-address)

❌ **Before:** Applicants must submit their documentation by the closing date. If an applicant misses the deadline, their application will not be considered.
✅ **After:** Submit your documentation by the closing date. If you miss the deadline, we will not consider your application.

*Address readers as `you`, not as `the applicant` or `individuals`.*

---

### Double negative (avoid-double-negatives)

❌ **Before:** It is not uncommon for applications to be not approved on the first submission.
✅ **After:** Applications are sometimes rejected on first submission.

*Two negatives cancel each other out but force extra mental effort. Restate positively.*

---

### Noun train (noun-train-limit)

❌ **Before:** The department has released a new human resource reallocation strategy implementation framework.
✅ **After:** The department has released a new framework for implementing its human resource reallocation strategy.

*Three or more stacked nouns before a head noun are hard to read. Break the chain with a prepositional phrase.*

---

### Jargon (avoid-jargon)

❌ **Before:** We need to leverage our cross-sector synergies to drive uplift in service delivery outcomes across our stakeholder ecosystem.
✅ **After:** We need to work with our partners to improve services.

*Remove jargon. Replace with the literal, specific meaning.*

---

## Inclusive Language

### 'Elderly' (age-avoid-elderly-old-people)

❌ **Before:** The program is designed for elderly Australians living independently.
✅ **After:** The program is designed for older Australians living independently.

*`Elderly` carries stereotyping connotations. Use `older people`, `older Australians`, or `seniors`.*

---

### 'he or she' instead of singular they (gender-singular-they)

❌ **Before:** If a customer has a complaint, he or she should contact the helpline.
✅ **After:** If a customer has a complaint, they should contact the helpline.

*Use singular `they` in generic contexts — not `he or she`, `his or her`, or `he/she`.*

---

### 'disabled person' instead of person-first language (disability-person-first-default)

❌ **Before:** Disabled people can access support through this service.
✅ **After:** People with disability can access support through this service.

*Person-first language by default: `person with disability`, not `disabled person`.*

---

### 'a disability' — article 'a' incorrect (disability-uncountable-noun)

❌ **Before:** People with a disability are encouraged to apply.
✅ **After:** People with disability are encouraged to apply.

*In Australian Government style, `disability` is an uncountable noun. No article 'a'.*

---

### Inspiration/hero framing (disability-no-inspiration-hero-victim)

❌ **Before:** Despite her disability, Sarah is an inspiration to us all and has overcome enormous adversity to become a researcher.
✅ **After:** Sarah is a researcher whose work has influenced national health policy.

*Focus on the person's actual role and achievements. Disability is not inherently inspirational, heroic, or tragic.*

---

### ATSI / Aborigines (atsi-avoid-shorthand-terms)

❌ **Before:** ATSI communities across the region were consulted during the planning process.
✅ **After:** Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander communities across the region were consulted during the planning process.

*Never use the acronym ATSI or the term `Aborigines`. Use `Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander peoples`, `First Nations people`, or a specific nation name.*

---

### First Nations peoples in past tense (atsi-use-present-tense)

❌ **Before:** The Ngunnawal people were the traditional custodians of the Canberra region and had a rich cultural connection to the land.
✅ **After:** The Ngunnawal people are the traditional custodians of the Canberra region and have a rich cultural connection to the land.

*Write about living peoples and cultures in the present tense. Past tense implies they no longer exist.*

---

### 'hearing impaired' (disability-deaf-hard-of-hearing)

❌ **Before:** Captions are available for hearing impaired viewers.
✅ **After:** Captions are available for viewers who are deaf or hard of hearing.

*`Hearing impaired` is not accepted. Use `person who is deaf or hard of hearing`.*

---

### 'Christian name' / 'surname' (cald-given-name-family-name)

❌ **Before:** Please enter your Christian name and surname in the fields below.
✅ **After:** Please enter your given name and family name in the fields below.

*`Christian name` assumes a religious tradition. `Surname` assumes European naming conventions. Use `given name` and `family name`.*

---

## Referencing

### Comma between author name and year (author-date-name-year-no-comma)

❌ **Before:** Recent research has confirmed these findings (Smith, 2021).
✅ **After:** Recent research has confirmed these findings (Smith 2021).

*No comma between the author's name and the year in author-date citations.*

---

### Ampersand instead of 'and' (author-date-two-authors-and-not-ampersand)

❌ **Before:** Several studies report similar outcomes (Brown & Chen 2020).
✅ **After:** Several studies report similar outcomes (Brown and Chen 2020).

*Use `and` — not `&` — between two author names in author-date citations.*

---

### ibid. in documentary notes (doc-note-no-latin-abbreviations)

❌ **Before:**
1. Diana Eades, *Aboriginal Ways of Using English*, Aboriginal Studies Press, 2013, p. 45.
2. ibid., p. 52.

✅ **After:**
1. Diana Eades, *Aboriginal Ways of Using English*, Aboriginal Studies Press, 2013, p. 45.
2. Eades, *Aboriginal Ways of Using English*, p. 52.

*Do not use `ibid.`, `op. cit.`, or `loc. cit.` Use author family name + short title instead.*

---

### Reference list heading (author-date-reflist-heading-references)

❌ **Before:** ## Bibliography
✅ **After:** ## References

*The reference list for author-date style must be headed `References`, not `Bibliography` or `Sources`.*

---

## Structuring

### Vague heading (headings-descriptive-not-vague)

❌ **Before:** ## More information
✅ **After:** ## Contact the grants team

*Headings must specifically describe the content beneath them. `More information` gives readers no signal.*

---

### Question-form heading (headings-no-questions)

❌ **Before:** ## How do I apply for the grant?
✅ **After:** ## Apply for a grant

*State the topic directly. Question-form headings push the key information to the end.*

---

### Generic link text (links-descriptive-text)

❌ **Before:** To find out more about the eligibility criteria, [click here](https://example.gov.au/eligibility).
✅ **After:** Read the [eligibility criteria for the grant](https://example.gov.au/eligibility).

*Link text must describe the destination when read in isolation. Not `click here`, `here`, or `read more`.*

---

### Table referenced by position (tables-cross-reference-in-text)

❌ **Before:** See the table below for a breakdown of outcomes by state.
✅ **After:** Table 3 shows a breakdown of outcomes by state.

*Reference tables by name or number — never `the table below` or `the table above`.*

---

### Run-on prose instead of a list (use-lists-for-series)

❌ **Before:** To apply, you will need to complete the application form and attach a copy of your passport and provide your tax file number and include two references and pay the $50 application fee.
✅ **After:** To apply, you will need to:
- complete the application form
- attach a copy of your passport
- provide your tax file number
- include two references
- pay the $50 application fee.

*When presenting 3 or more related items or steps, use a list rather than run-on prose.*
