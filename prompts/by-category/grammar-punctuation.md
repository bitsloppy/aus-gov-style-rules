# AGSM Grammar and Punctuation — System Prompt Drop-in

> Compiled from stylemanual.gov.au · 2026-09-22

---

## Apostrophes

- Singular noun possession: apostrophe + s — **committee's report**, **bus's passengers** (even when the noun ends in s).
- Plural nouns ending in s: apostrophe only — **both committees' reports**.
- Possessive pronouns never take an apostrophe: **its**, **yours**, **theirs**, **ours**, **hers**, **whose**.
- No apostrophe to form the plural of nouns, decade names, or acronyms: **1980s**, **MPs**, **PCs** — not 1980's, MP's.
- Exception: single letters and digits pluralise with apostrophe + s — **i's and t's**, **0's and 1's**.
- Official Australian place names have no apostrophe: **Kings Cross**, not King's Cross.
- **it's** = it is / it has. **its** = possessive. Never confuse them.

## Brackets

- Brackets enclose non-essential text that can be removed without changing meaning.
- Square brackets mark editorial insertions or paraphrases inside a quotation.
- [sic] appears in square brackets (not round) to flag errors in quoted text.
- Nested parenthetical: use square brackets inside round — never double round brackets.

## Colons

- Use a colon to introduce a list when the lead-in could stand alone.
- Do not use a colon after a verb where the list items complete the sentence naturally.
- First word after a colon is lowercase unless it is a proper noun or opens a complete question.
- Ratios use a colon with no surrounding spaces: **50:50** not 50 : 50.

## Commas

- Place a comma after an introductory phrase or adverbial clause.
- Enclose non-essential clauses with a matched pair of commas.
- **No Oxford comma** in simple lists unless needed to prevent ambiguity.
- Add an Oxford comma when the last item is itself a compound joined by *and*.
- No comma splice — do not join two independent sentences with only a comma.
- Four-digit-plus numbers use commas: **6,500**, **745,902,350** — not spaces.
- No comma after Latin abbreviations inside parentheses: **(e.g. lithium)** not (e.g., lithium).

## Dashes

- Parenthetical asides: **spaced en dash** (–) — not an unspaced em dash (—).
- Coordinate nouns: en dash, not hyphen — **Murray–Darling Basin**, **student–teacher ratio**.
- Financial year spans: **2022–23** (en dash, four-digit / two-digit, unspaced).
- Number or date spans in body text: prefer **from … to** or **between … and** over an en dash.

## Ellipsis

- Use the single ellipsis character **…** not three separate dots `...`.
- Place a single space before and after an ellipsis.
- Do not follow an ellipsis with a full stop, comma, or semicolon.

## Exclamation marks

- Avoid in formal government content (reports, briefs, official correspondence).
- One exclamation mark only — never two or more consecutively.

## Full stops

- Every declarative sentence ends with a full stop.
- No full stop after a standalone URL or email address.
- No full stop inside hyperlink text — place it after the closing tag.
- No full stop at the end of headings, captions, or unit symbols.

## Hyphens

- Hyphenate compound adjectives before a noun; drop the hyphen in predicative position: **up-to-date accounts** but **the accounts are up to date**.
- Hyphenate a prefix that ends in the same vowel as the base word begins: **de-emphasise**, **re-enter**, **pre-eminent**.
- Hyphenate a prefix attached to a word beginning with a capital: **un-Australian**.
- Always hyphenate **ex-** meaning *former*: **ex-president**.
- No hyphen when an adverb ending in **-ly** modifies a participle: **fully loaded**, **rapidly declining**. Exceptions: fully-fashioned, fully-fledged.
- Written numbers 21–99: **twenty-one**, **thirty-four**.

## Quotation marks

- Primary quotation marks: **single** ('like this'). Double marks for quotations within quotations.
- Block quotations (approximately 30+ words): indented, no quotation marks.
- Punctuation belonging to the quoted text goes inside the closing mark.
- Only one end punctuation mark when the quotation and sentence marks conflict.

## Semicolons

- Avoid using semicolons to link independent clauses — use a full stop or comma + conjunction instead.
- Never end a list item with a semicolon.
- Use semicolons to separate items in a run-on sentence list where items contain commas.

## Abbreviations and acronyms

- No full stop after most abbreviations (misc, para, vol, dept, approx).
- Do not begin a sentence with an abbreviation.
- Define acronyms and initialisms on first use: **Department of Social Services (DSS)**.
- No full stops within acronyms: **ATO** not A.T.O.
- Plural of an acronym: plain lowercase s — **LGAs** not LGA's.
- Contractions (Dr, Ltd, Pty) take no trailing full stop mid-sentence.

## Spelling

- Follow one Australian English dictionary consistently — Macquarie or Australian Concise Oxford.
- Use the first-listed variant: **acknowledgement** not acknowledgment.
