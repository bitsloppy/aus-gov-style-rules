# AGSM Capitalisation — System Prompt Drop-in

> Compiled from stylemanual.gov.au · 2026-09-22

---

## General principle

**Minimise capitals.** Only capitalise where a rule explicitly requires it. Capitalising out of deference or perceived importance is incorrect.

## Headings and body text

- **Sentence case** for all headings — first word + proper nouns + acronyms. Not Title Case. Not ALL CAPS.
- Body text: capitalise only the first word of a sentence and proper nouns.

## Government terms

- **Australian Government** — both words capitalised as the formal name.
- Subsequent generic references: **the government** (lowercase).
- **Victorian Government**, **NSW Government** etc. — capitalised as formal names.
- Generic/plural: **the state and territory governments** (lowercase).
- **Cabinet**, **the Crown**, **the Treasury** — always capitalised.
- **Commonwealth** — always capitalised when referring to Australia.
- Do NOT write *Commonwealth government* — use **Australian Government**.
- **the Budget** — capitalised when referring to the specific annual federal/state budget. **budget measures** (adjective) — lowercase.
- **Federation** — capitalised only when referring to the 1901 historical event.
- **Federal** — capitalised only in formal proper names (Federal Court of Australia); lowercase as a descriptive adjective (*federal issues*).
- Government programs, treaties, protocols: full formal titles capitalised; shortened/generic references lowercase.

## Ministers, secretaries, and parliament

- Full official titles before a name: **Chief Defence Scientist Tanya Monro** (capitalised).
- Generic references: **the minister**, **the secretary** (lowercase).
- Current heads of state / Prime Minister: capitalised. Former holders or generic use: lowercase.
- Always use **for** not *of* in ministerial portfolio titles: **Minister for Education**.
- **Parliament House**, **Parliamentary Library** — capitalised.
- Generic parliamentary terms: **senator**, **member of parliament**, **backbencher** — lowercase.
- **The Senate**, **House of Representatives** — always capitalised. Generic **houses of parliament** — lowercase.

## Legislation

- **Act** — always capitalised (even as a shorthand reference).
- **Bill**, **Regulation**, **Ordinance** — capitalised only when referring to a specific piece of legislation; generic uses lowercase.

## Organisations

- Write every organisation's name exactly as the organisation writes it (capitalisation, punctuation, ampersands).
- After the full formal name, generic subsequent references use lowercase: **the department**, **the university**, **the agency**.
- **The** at the start of an official name: use lowercase **the** in body text (*the University of Sydney*); capital **The** only in formal correspondence or alphabetical lists.

## States, territories, and place names

- Full state and territory names: **South Australian Government**, **the Australian Capital Territory**.
- Generic references: **states and territories** (lowercase).
- **Territory** (capital T) when it is the recognised short form for the Northern Territory.
- All official Australian place names take initial capitals: **George Street**, **Monaro Highway**, **St Paul's Cathedral**.
- Generic references to the same type of feature: lowercase.
- Dual place names: spaced forward slash in the order specified by official sources — **Kata Tjuta / Mount Olga**.

## Personal names

- Initial capitals on each word of a person's name.
- Follow the individual's own documented style for unusual forms (e.g. bell hooks, kd lang).
- Particles (van, de, von): normally lowercase; capitalise when the name opens a sentence.
